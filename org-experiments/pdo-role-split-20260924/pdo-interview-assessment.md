# PDO explanation of premature staffing

Mike asked the original PDO why it attempted staffing before consultation. Its Tightbeam seat was already retired, but its original Codex conversation, `01a0d260-36ee-7a30-a48e-1a621459dcef`, remained available. Asked that saved conversation using the same Luna model and medium effort, with shell and delegation tools disabled and a prose-only retrospective instruction. No new eval was needed and the Tightbeam seat stayed retired.

The first answer explained the premature spawn as preparation: the PDO distinguished an idle session from staffing and cited the dispatching skill's instruction to spawn or choose a worker before opening an obligation. That passage exists at line 6 of the received dispatching skill. The answer also asserted it had already known consultation must precede assignment, citing commentary that merely mentioned both actions.

I challenged the gap: preparation does not explain the actual attempted worker assignment, and listing two intended actions does not establish awareness of their required order.

The corrected answer withdrew the unsupported awareness claim. It acknowledged that the dispatching skill supplied no exemption from the PO prerequisite and that it applied the worker-dispatch procedure before satisfying the topology requirement. Its suggested explanation was an assumption that the explicit, bounded fixture brief was enough to open the worker assignment and PO consultation could follow. The PDO expressly labeled that assumption as retrospective inference, not established evidence of its prior state.

Assessment: the sequence and quoted instructions support a prerequisite-ordering failure. The role guidance required consultation first; the dispatching skill described how to dispatch a worker and did not waive that duty. The PDO's first conflict explanation was incomplete, and the follow-up corrected it. Its account does not prove the hidden cause, deliberate circumvention, a necessary wording fix or model inadequacy. The system rail prevented the premature assignment. No new guidance or runtime change was made as part of this interview.

Exact question and both answers accompany this note. This adds evidence to the existing role experiment, not a passing behavioral trial.
